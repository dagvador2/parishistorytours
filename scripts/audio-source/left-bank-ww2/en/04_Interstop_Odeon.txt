You're now standing in front of the Théâtre de l'Odéon. Look at the left-hand side of the building. You should see a small commemorative plaque set into the stone.

This plaque is dedicated to Jacques Guierre, an FFI fighter — that's the Forces Françaises de l'Intérieur, the interior resistance forces — who was killed on this square at the age of twenty, on the morning of the twenty-fifth of August nineteen forty-four.

It's a fitting introduction to what's coming next. The FFI didn't fight a conventional battle. Their role during the insurrection was to disrupt the Germans as much as possible — and crucially, to gather intelligence and pass it to the Allies, so that the French and American armies could enter Paris with some idea of what they were walking into.

Jacques Guierre was barely twenty years old. He died on this square, on this very morning. The plaque is the only mark of it.
