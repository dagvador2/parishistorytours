Their role during the insurrection, which would lead to the liberation of Paris, was to disrupt the Germans as much as possible, and above all to gather intelligence and pass it to the Allies, so that the French and American armies could enter Paris with some idea of what they were walking into.

That morning, Jacques Guierre had orders to gather intelligence on German movements around the Luxembourg Gardens. Crossing this square, on his way to the gardens, he was shot by a German soldier.
