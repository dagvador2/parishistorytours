The First World War ends in nineteen eighteen. The Germans sign the armistice inside a railway carriage in the forest of Compiègne. The following year, they are forced to sign the Treaty of Versailles — a treaty that brings Germany to its knees.

The treaty hit Germany on three fronts. Territorial: Germany lost fifteen percent of its land and all its colonies. Financial: a hundred and thirty-two billion gold marks in reparations — the equivalent of forty-seven kilotons of gold, or three times the current annual budget of the French state. And military: no more armoured vehicles, no air force, and the army capped at one hundred thousand men.

These sanctions stirred a powerful desire for revenge in Germany. The nineteen twenty-nine crash drove poverty and unemployment through the roof. Fascism spread. And Hitler seized power in January nineteen thirty-three, exactly ten years after his failed putsch attempt. Once in power, he started tearing up the Treaty of Versailles piece by piece — refusing to pay what was owed, and above all rebuilding the German army.

France and the United Kingdom responded with pacifism. The central figure here is the British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain. He wanted to preserve peace at almost any cost. Every time Hitler broke a clause of the treaty, the French and the British said: we're not going to war over this. Peace matters more.

This way of thinking peaked at the Munich Agreement, in September nineteen thirty-eight. Hitler, Chamberlain, France and Italy shook hands over a region of Czechoslovakia called the Sudetenland — a part of the country with a large German-speaking population. Hitler was given the green light to take it. Chamberlain came home waving the agreement, proud of what he called peace for our time. Six months later, Hitler broke the treaty and invaded the rest of Czechoslovakia by force.

Finally, the French and the British changed their minds. They decided Poland was the red line Hitler could not cross. And when Hitler invaded Poland anyway on the first of September nineteen thirty-nine, France and the United Kingdom declared war two days later, on the third of September.
