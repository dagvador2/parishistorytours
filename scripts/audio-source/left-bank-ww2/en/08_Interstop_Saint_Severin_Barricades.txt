You're now in front of the Église Saint-Séverin, one of the oldest churches on the Left Bank, on Rue du Petit-Pont.

Take a look at the street you're standing on. In the PDF, you'll find a photograph taken at this exact spot in August nineteen forty-four. The same street, eighty years apart.

What you would have seen here in August nineteen forty-four was a barricade. More than six hundred barricades were built across Paris during the insurrection week. They were made of cobblestones, café tables, café chairs, café signs, mattresses, doors — anything heavy enough to slow a German tank.

The Germans had about twenty-two thousand men and one hundred tanks in Paris that week. The barricades were a logistical nightmare for them. Every time they tried to move armour across the city, they had to send infantry first to dismantle blockages, often under fire from the windows above.
